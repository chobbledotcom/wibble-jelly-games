      ## Where these ideas came from

      Fete Fun was a website I wrote for school and village fetes. It went live on 10 December 2002, after a friend asked for the instructions for the balloon bursting game, and the last update went up on 30 November 2004. The domain no longer serves a site, so the useful parts are reproduced here. The [original pages are in the Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20041205105104/http://www.nforbes.plus.com/fetefun/index.htm).

      The site was a catalogue of stalls, games and sideshows for school and village fetes. Each one had a note on popularity, profitability, how much work it took to set up and how many adults it needed. Some of the ideas were tried at our own fetes. Some were collected because they looked like they would work. That was the point of the site, and it is still the honest description of the notes below.

      I have lightly edited the spelling and punctuation, dropped the old supplier list, and removed the gendered labels from the age ranges. I have not added new games or changed how any of them work. Prices and prize values are as written in 2004.

      ## Using the ratings

      The original site rated each game from 1 to 3. For popularity and profitability, 1 is low and 3 is high. For setup, 1 is easy and 3 is hard work. Staff is the original estimate of adults needed; 0.5 means one adult could look after the stall alongside another. A dash means the original site did not rate that game. A star marks one of the original site's favourites.

      ## The games, A to Z

      ### Baked beans bath

      The volunteer sits in a bathtub or paddling pool and has baked beans poured over them. Use safety goggles, as beans have a high salt content and will sting the eyes. This works best if the person in the bath is a person of authority such as the school Head Teacher. Additional money can be raised if sponsorship is collected before the event. Invite the local press photographer along, as they like this type of event and there is a good chance the picture will appear in the local paper. Variations include custard or any other gunge-like substance in place of the baked beans.

      ### Balloon burst

      A solenoid holding a dart is slid down two wires strung between a tall post and a short post. As the solenoid slides down the wires it passes over a balloon on the ground. The contestant presses a button, releasing the dart, and attempts to pop the balloon. A prize is won if the balloon is popped. A very popular game, as it is harder than it looks to pop the balloon.

      ### Bauble hunt

      Make or purchase baubles to hang on a Christmas tree. Hide a number in each bauble, pick the bauble off the tree and win a prize. For a cheaper version, decorate square envelopes to look like Christmas presents and hang these from the tree in place of the baubles.

      ### Bear's bungee jump

      The customers bring their teddy bears (or other favourite toy animals) and these are thrown off the top of the church steeple attached to a bungee cord. The name of each bear is recorded on a certificate, which is presented to the bear's owner after the jump. This event was very popular at our local church when run as part of the church fete. A lot of adults produced some very battered but much-loved bears to jump alongside their children's bears, making it a good family event.

      ### Beat the goalie

      Set up a football goal with a goalie, allow three kicks per contestant. Award prizes for each football kicked past the goalie.

      ### Bottle tombola

      Ask for donations of bottles (such as shampoo, soft drinks, wine or alcohol) then stick a number on each bottle. Use tickets from one half of a raffle ticket book. Depending on the odds you wish to offer, either stick every number on a bottle (100% chance of winning) or just use every fifth ticket (1 in 5 chance of winning). Fold up the other half of the raffle tickets and put them in a box suitable for people to draw out of. If a child wins an alcoholic prize then hand the prize to their parent. Alternatively run two bottle tombolas, one for children and one for adults, and charge more for the adults' tombola. If the quality or quantity of the donations is not high then you may need to buy some bottles to make the stall more attractive. Take care not to buy too many expensive bottles.

      ### Bouncy castle

      A big inflatable castle or inflatable animal for children to jump on. Do not expect to make lots of money from a bouncy castle, as they are expensive to hire. However, they usually break even and should be considered a loss leader. No school fete is complete without a bouncy castle. As they are big, they often stick up above buildings and help to draw in the crowds.

      ### Bucket of water coin drop

      Fill a large tub with water and place coins on the bottom. The contestant drops coins into the water and if they completely cover a coin on the bottom they win a prize. This game is a lot more difficult than it looks.

      ### Cake and bake stall

      Put prices on all of the cakes and baking donated by parents and place them on a table.

      ### Chocolate tombola

      Similar to the bottle tombola but use chocolate. Ask for donations of chocolate then stick a number on each bar. Use tickets from one half of a raffle ticket book. Depending on the odds you wish to offer, either stick every number on a bar (100% chance of winning) or just use every fifth ticket (1 in 5 chance of winning). Fold up the other half of the raffle tickets and put them in a box suitable for people to draw out of. If the quality or quantity of the donations is not high then you may need to buy some chocolate to make the stall more attractive. Take care not to buy too many expensive bars.

      ### Coconut shy

      Set up the coconuts on the stands and allow three throws of a wooden ball per contestant. The contestants win any coconuts they knock off the stands. Use netting (a football goal works well) to contain the thrown balls.

      ### Count sweets in the jar

      Fill a jar with sweets, counting the number used as the jar is filled. Keep the total secret by writing it on a piece of paper and sealing it in an envelope. Record the name and contact details when a guess is made; the winner gets the jar of sweets. It is also possible to use a jar of beans and then offer something else as a prize.

      ### Cow pat sweepstake

      Mark out a field into squares, sell the squares and record the "owner" of each square. Put a cow in the field and award the prize to the "owner" of the square where the cow produces the first cow pat. It could also be played with a sheep or goat, or for a more manageable version use a mouse or hamster in a cage.

      ### Darts

      Set up a dartboard, give each contestant three darts and award prizes depending on the number of points scored with three darts. Alternatively set up a dartboard covered with pictures of local dignitaries, cartoon characters or members of the school staff and award prizes for the pictures hit.

      ### Duck racing

      Each contestant buys a numbered toy bath duck. The ducks are dropped into a stream to start the race and the first duck past the winning line is the winner. Collect the ducks up so they can be reused or given to the contestants.

      ### Duck shoot

      Set up a row of toy bath ducks on a stand and give the contestant a water pistol to try to shoot the ducks off the stand. Award prizes for each duck shot.

      ### Egg and spoon race

      Each contestant is given a spoon and a hard-boiled egg. The egg must be carried on the spoon, as fast as possible, along the course. If run as a fete attraction, try using an obstacle course and divide the contestants into age groups. Times can be recorded using a stopwatch and prizes awarded either for the winner in each age group or for completing the course within predefined time limits.

      ### Face painting

      Children's faces (or adults') are painted to look like cartoon characters or animals. Obtain sets of face paints from party shops or children's stores. Try to use someone with artistic talent to carry out the face painting.

      ### Fire engine darts

      Place pieces of paper with the contestant's contact details on the ground; the papers may need weighting down with rocks or sand. Drop a large dart from the top of the fire engine's extendable ladder. The contestant closest to where the dart lands is the winner.

      ### Grand prize draw

      Obtain donations of prizes from local companies and sell pre-printed draw tickets both in advance and at the fete. Hold the draw at the end of the fete. A gaming or lottery licence may be required.

      ### Greasy pole pillow fight

      Can be held using either a pole over a swimming pool or a long bench on gym tumble mats. Two contestants stand on the pole or bench and try to knock the other contestant off using a pillow. Wet the pole or bench to make the game more challenging.

      ### Greasy watermelon scramble

      Best played in a swimming pool. Cover a watermelon in lard or another edible grease and allow two teams to scramble for the watermelon. Points are scored for each goal. At the end of the game the teams share the watermelon.

      ### Guess bear's birthday

      The object is to guess the birthday of the bear (or other cuddly toy). The person who correctly guesses the date, or comes closest to it, wins the bear. Not a great money earner but popular with small children and their grandparents, and a useful way of raising money using toys donated by department stores or toy shops.

      ### Guess bear's name

      The object is to guess the name of the bear (or other cuddly toy). The person who correctly guesses the name, or comes closest to it, wins the bear. Not a great money earner but popular with small children and their grandparents, and a useful way of raising money using toys donated by department stores or toy shops.

      ### Guess baby's birth weight

      If a new baby is available (a couple of days old is best), put them on display and charge for a guess at the birth weight. Award a prize for the correct or nearest answer and record the guesses on a piece of paper marked up in 1 oz or 50 gram increments. This can also be held before the baby is born: the mum-to-be sticks a notice advertising the contest on her bump and walks around the fete collecting guesses.

      ### Gunge

      **Nomination gunging.** This works by using a vote system. You have several people up for nomination. They could be bosses, teachers or anyone. Over a week or so, people vote for who they want to see gunged. Charge a set amount per vote; 20p votes can raise £500 in a senior school teacher gunging. At the end of the voting period, the winner (or loser, as is more appropriate) gets gunged.

      **Auction gunging.** When the person to be gunged has been selected, auction off the privilege of pouring the gunge to the highest bidder. If someone has a particular grudge against the person up for nomination, they will pay surprising amounts to be the one to cover them in gunge.

      **Gunge bombs.** Divide people into teams, each having a flag to protect. Each team has a little stock of gunge bombs. Teams take it in turns to try to get the enemy's flag. For charity events you can make teams pay to enter, or make each individual player pay every time they are hit. This game could be described as a less painful, more gungy version of paintball.

      ### Hook a duck / boat

      Float a number of toy bath ducks or boats in a child's paddling pool. Place a star on the bottom of one of the ducks or boats and give the contestants either a pole with a hook or a child's fishing net. Award prizes for the ducks or boats caught, with a special prize for the one with a star.

      ### Hoopla

      Arrange the prizes on a table and provide hoops for the contestants to try to throw over them. A very useful game for getting rid of donated fast-food-chain free toys. Vary the criteria for what counts as a successful throw depending on how many prizes you want to get rid of: hoop partially on the prize, or fully on. Be consistent. It also works well with chocolate prizes. If the prizes are too large for a hoop to fit over, use something smaller as a target and mark it to indicate the prize to be won.

      ### Horse shoes

      Set up a throwing area with stakes at increasing distances from the throwing line. Give the contestant three horseshoes to throw and award prizes based on the stake the horseshoe lands around.

      ### Human fruit machine

      Three large boxes are set up with a person (child) in each box. On a signal, each person randomly picks a piece of fruit and holds it out of a hole in the front of the box. If all the pieces of fruit are the same, the player wins a prize. This is a good event for children to help run and is not often seen at fetes, so it has novelty value.

      ### Jumble sale

      Collect donations of jumble, price it up and display it on a table. Use any donations that are unlikely to sell for trash or treasure.

      ### Lucky dip

      Purchase small toys and wrap them in tissue paper, then place them in a box filled with packing peanuts or sawdust. For a Christmas fete, use Christmas paper and a box decorated in a Christmas theme.

      ### Mine field / mouse trap game

      Set up mouse traps (at least eight) in a fenced-off area. Throw tennis balls at the traps; if a trap is set off a prize is won, such as a fun-size chocolate bar. Put exploding caps on each trap for added effect. Use netting as the fence to prevent the balls rolling away. Originally seen as a means to raise funds for a mine clearance charity.

      ### Obstacle course

      Set up a course using scrambling nets, barrels, climbing frames and balancing beams for the children (or adults) to climb over. Charge for each circuit of the course.

      ### Outside traders

      Arrange for small traders to sell their goods at the fete, charging either a table fee or a percentage of takings. Handmade toys, cards, chocolates, candles, clothes and similar goods usually sell well. Consult the traders in advance to ensure you can provide any tables or mains power they require. If the fete is outside, you may need marquees or other suitable shelter. Try your local Avon Lady and ask all the traders if they know of others who would like to take part.

      ### Panning for gold

      Place a layer of sand approximately 25 mm deep in a plastic storage tub and seed the sand with gold nuggets purchased from a fete supplies company. Fill the rest of the tub with water. Each contestant pans for gold using a baking dish with holes drilled in the bottom. Weigh the nuggets panned by the contestant and award a certificate or prize. Recycle the nuggets for the next contestant.

      ### Pick an egg

      Fill a tray with sand and place upturned eggshells in the sand. If the shell picked has a dot inside it, the contestant wins a small prize.

      ### Pick a lolly

      Obtain an empty fruit tray from the supermarket, decorate the box, turn it upside down and poke lots of holes in the bottom. Put a red dot on the bottom of a few lollies and then stick all of the lollies into the holes. A contestant picks a lolly and, if it has a dot on the bottom, wins an additional small prize.

      ### Ping pong ball throw

      Stand tubes of different lengths on end (sections of tube from carpet shops work well) and place small prizes in each tube. The contestant throws a ping pong ball at the tubes and wins the prize if the ball falls into a tube.

      ### Plate smash

      Arrange unwanted crockery on a stand and charge people to throw three wooden balls at it. Obtain the crockery cheaply from charity shops. Arrange netting to catch the balls and set up the crockery smash in an area where the broken bits can easily be swept up.

      ### Pocket money toys

      Buy pocket money toys in bulk from a fete supply company or another toy wholesaler, display them on a table and sell them for a profit. Not all of the toys will sell, so be prepared to store some until the next fete. Use any left-over toys as prizes in the lucky dip or Santa's grotto.

      ### Pony rides

      Charge for children to sit on a pony while it is led around a field or playground.

      ### Putt for points

      Set up a long board with a number of holes cut into its bottom edge, so that a golf ball may be putted into the holes. Allocate points to each hole depending on its size. Award prizes based on the number of points scored.

      ### Quizzes

      Quizzes are always popular, as they can be easily copied and sent home with each child, or used on a quiz night. For a themed quiz, set all of the questions around the chosen theme.

      ### Quoits

      Numbered stakes are set into the ground and the contestant tries to throw rope rings around them. Prizes are awarded based on the number of points scored.

      ### Raffle

      Obtain donations of prizes from local businesses. Sell raffle tickets on the day to everyone attending the fete. At the end of the fete, conduct a random draw and allocate the prizes in the order the tickets are drawn. A gaming or lottery licence may be required.

      ### Refreshment stall

      Sell soft drinks, tea, mulled wine (an alcohol licence may be required) and slices of cake, using some of the cakes and baking donated by parents. It may be possible to give an alcoholic drink away as part of the entry fee without an alcohol licence; check with your local authorities.

      ### Rudolph beanbag throw

      A cartoon character is drawn on a piece of cardboard or MDF approximately 1.2 m by 0.6 m, with a large hole for its mouth. The contestant throws beanbags at the character and prizes are awarded for beanbags thrown into the mouth. Either award prizes depending on how many beanbags go through the mouth, or give out a sweet to each contestant. This attraction does not usually make much money but is fun, especially for young children. A more advanced version has a switch in the mouth that lights Rudolph's nose each time a beanbag enters. For a summer fete, replace Rudolph with a clown.

      ### Sack race

      Stand contestants in hessian sacks and watch them shuffle or hop down the course. For added amusement, use a simple obstacle course rather than a flat grass course.

      ### Sand art

      Obtain coloured sand and plastic bottles from a fete supplier. Participants create a colourful bottle of sand by pouring each of the coloured sands into the bottle to make layers of colour.

      ### Santa's grotto

      An absolute must for all Christmas fetes. Set up a small room or screened-off area with suitable Christmas decorations and charge admission for children to tell Santa what they want for Christmas. Include a small gift; for an additional charge, provide a photo of the child with Santa. The small gifts should be wrapped in Christmas paper and divided by age range. Remember to allow for the cost of the gifts in the admission price. The photo needs to be produced immediately, so use a Polaroid camera or a digital camera and printer.

      ### Shove half penny - giant

      A giant version of the regular shove half penny game.

      ### Snowmen skittles

      Snowmen skittles is a Christmas-themed version of skittles. The object is to bowl the ball at the skittles and knock over as many as possible. Each contestant gets three throws and prizes are either awarded for knocking over all of the skittles or just for taking part. Decorate ten plastic bottles to look like snowmen and use a child's ball to knock them over. Either award prizes depending on how many skittles are knocked over, or give out a sweet to each contestant.

      ### Spin the wheel

      Use a circular board divided into eight colours with a spinning arrow. The contestant picks a colour and, if the arrow stops on it, wins a prize. Alternatively, place a prize on each colour and the contestant wins the prize the arrow points to.

      ### Splat the rat / alien

      A classic fete game. A section of drainage pipe is mounted vertically on a table and the contestant tries to splat the beanbag rat or alien with a bat as it emerges from the pipe. The rat dropper counts down from three each time they drop a rat. Allow each contestant three rats and award each contestant a lolly.

      ### Tin can target practice

      Use either wooden balls or a large super soaker water pistol to knock over stacks of empty cans. If wooden balls are used, some sort of netting or backstop is required to prevent injury.

      ### Trash or treasure

      Not a huge money earner but very popular with children. Choose items from the incoming donated jumble for the trash or treasure stall. Remember, one person's trash is the next person's treasure, so include a good mixture of books, toys, games and free toys from fast-food chains. Wrap everything in newspaper to hide what it is and charge a small amount for each item chosen. This is very useful for getting rid of donated jumble that will never sell on a jumble stall. Be prepared for a mad scramble, as children love this stall, and arrange to have a bin close by for the newspaper. If the child is not happy with what they have chosen, suggest they use it as a Christmas present for someone, intercept anything being thrown into the bin, and offer it for sale again unwrapped.

      ### Treasure map

      Draw a map of a treasure island on a large piece of paper, including sketches of palm trees and sea monsters, then draw a grid over the paper. Number the squares and record the numbers of the squares that contain treasure (small prizes or sweets) on a hidden piece of paper. Contestants pick a square by drawing an X on it and win any treasure in that square.

      ### Used car smash

      Obtain an old wreck from a scrap dealer and set it up on hard standing. Charge people to use a sledgehammer on the car, or a nail to scratch the paint. Charge extra to break windows. Provide goggles and gloves. Get the scrap dealer to collect the car at the end of the fete and then sweep up the bits.

      ### Wet sponge throw

      The volunteer sits on a chair or in a set of stocks and you charge people to throw three wet sponges at them. Provide the volunteer with goggles to prevent eye injuries. This event is best held on a warm summer's day, not in the cold of winter.

      ### Wiggly wire game

      Bend stiff wire to produce a course for the contestant to move a hoop along. Wire a buzzer to the wiggly wire and the hoop so that, if the hoop touches the wire, a circuit is made and the buzzer sounds.

The same applies here. Use your own judgement, follow the safety and licensing rules for your event, and do not run anything you are not confident you can operate safely.
