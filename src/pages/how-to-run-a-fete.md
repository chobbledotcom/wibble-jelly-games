      ## Start with the date

      Pick the date and time carefully. Avoid clashing with other Christmas events, school concerts and the diary of the hall or field you want to use.

      For a school fete, either start as lessons finish or hold it in the evening or at the weekend. A fete that opens half an hour after the school day ends will be poorly attended: people will not wait around in the cold and wet, and once they get home they will not come back out.

      The same applies to the end. Give people a reason to stay for an hour rather than ten minutes. Food and drink help. So does having something for different ages to do.

      ## Book the help and the licences

      Appoint a fete organiser and a treasurer early. Give other volunteers clear jobs: planning and building Santa's grotto, writing to companies for donations, sorting jumble, running the tombola.

      Apply for any gaming, lottery or alcohol licences now. They can take weeks.

      Send letters to local companies asking for donations of services or raffle prizes. Invite outside traders and decide whether you will charge a table fee or a percentage of takings.

      Put the date in the school or organisation's calendar and in the regular newsletter. Start a list of the stalls you want and check four things for each one:

      - Does it need to be built or prepared in advance?
      - Does it need supplies bought in advance?
      - Will there be enough people to run it?
      - Is there room for it in the hall, field or building?

      ## The countdown

      ### Months in advance

      Gather the big items for Santa's grotto: a tree, decorations, an armchair for Santa and a sleigh made from cardboard or MDF. If someone can paint, add a set of reindeer.

      Make a life-size snowman by covering space hoppers with papier-mâché. Once it has dried, deflate the space hoppers and remove them. Stack three of the resulting balls, paint them white, and add a carrot nose, eyes, mouth, scarf and hat.

      Ask the children to make things to sell: laminated table mats, handmade Christmas cards, decorations.

      ### One month to go

      Confirm the stalls and who is running each one. Check that any games needing a build, a stand or a power supply are on schedule.

      Arrange for donations of bottles, chocolate, cakes, jumble and tombola prizes. Different year groups can be allocated different items so you do not end up with forty bottles of shampoo and no chocolate.

      ### Two weeks to go

      Invite the local paper to the fete. Free publicity is useful, and a photograph of the head teacher in a baked bean bath tends to get printed.

      Send out flyers listing the major attractions and the outside traders. Put up posters.

      If the school normally has a uniform, allow pupils to wear their own clothes in return for a tombola prize or a jumble item. Alternatively accept a small cash donation.

      Sort the incoming donations. Number the tombola prizes. Split jumble into ordinary jumble, trash or treasure and hoopla prizes. Wrap the trash or treasure in newspaper. Wrap the lucky dip prizes in Christmas paper.

      Start filling a large box for each stall with its signs, instructions, prizes and float.

      ### One to two weeks to go

      Decorate Santa's grotto if the room is available.

      The treasurer should prepare a float for each stall and put it in a labelled plastic container, such as a takeaway box. Keep a record of how much cash each stall starts with.

      Send a second flyer. Make it different from the first so that people read it rather than throwing it away.

      ### On the day

      If the school normally has a uniform, allow pupils to wear their own clothes in return for cakes or baking. Alternatively accept a small cash donation.

      Sort the cakes into those for the cake stall and those to be sold by the slice with tea and mulled wine.

      Start setting up as early as possible. It always takes longer than expected.

      ### After the fete

      Send a thank-you letter to every company that donated services or raffle prizes. Tell them how much was raised and what it is helping to fund. You will be asking again next year.

      Some businesses only donate every two or three years. Note the year of each donation so you do not waste a request.

      The treasurer should produce a summary of each stall's profit, allowing for the cost of running it. That summary is the most useful document for planning the next fete.

      ## The money

      Not every stall has to make a profit. Santa's grotto and the bouncy castle may need to be run as loss leaders to pull people in. With care, most stalls should at least break even.

      A fete raises money and raises the profile of the organisation. If people are having fun, they spend money. Tombolas may make the most, but a fete with only tombolas and none of the usual games will only attract people for a short stay. The longer people stay, the more likely they are to want a drink and something to eat. Food sales can be very profitable, especially if the cakes have been donated.

      ## A Christmas theme

      A Christmas tree, tinsel and coloured paper will transform a dull hall quickly. Most of the games can be given a Christmas feel with a bit of decoration:

      - Use Christmas wrapping paper for the lucky dip.
      - Decorate plastic bottles as snowmen for skittles.
      - Hang numbered baubles on a tree for a bauble hunt.
      - Replace the summer clown with Rudolph for a beanbag throw.
      - Add tinsel to the tombola and hoopla tables.

      ## Stalls worth having

      These are the stalls the original Fete Fun guide recommended for a Christmas fete. Each one has fuller instructions on the [fete game ideas](/fete-game-ideas/) page.

      - **Santa's grotto.** The one thing a Christmas fete cannot do without. Charge admission, include a small gift, and offer a photograph for an extra charge.
      - **Outside traders.** Charge a table fee or a percentage. Handmade toys, cards, chocolates, candles and clothes sell well. Ask what tables and power they need.
      - **Cake and baking stall.** Price up the donated cakes and put them on a table.
      - **Refreshments.** Soft drinks, tea, mulled wine and cake by the slice. Check whether you need an alcohol licence.
      - **Trash or treasure.** Wrap jumble in newspaper and charge a small amount per item. Very popular with children and a good way to clear the jumble that will not sell.
      - **Bottle tombola and chocolate tombola.** Number the prizes and sell tickets. Decide the odds before you start.
      - **Hoopla.** Useful for getting rid of donated toys and chocolate.
      - **Lucky dip.** Wrap small toys in Christmas paper.
      - **Bauble hunt.** Hide numbers in baubles on a tree.
      - **Snowmen skittles.** Ten decorated bottles and a child's ball.
      - **Rudolph beanbag throw.** A painted board with a hole for the mouth.

      The full list on the [fete game ideas](/fete-game-ideas/) page includes the ratings for popularity, profitability, setup and staffing, plus notes for more than sixty games.

      ## One last check

      Walk the site before the doors open. Check that every stall has an adult in charge, that cables and guy ropes are out of walkways, that water and grease are away from the electrics, and that there is a first-aid point people can find. If something does not look safe, change it or close it. The fete is not worth an injury.
